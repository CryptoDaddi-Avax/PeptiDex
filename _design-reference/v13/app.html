Retatrutide (LY3437943) is an investigational once-weekly triple-agonist peptide acting simultaneously on the GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon receptors. By engaging all three incretin pathways it suppresses appetite, improves insulin sensitivity, and increases energy expenditure — a mechanism distinct from single- and dual-agonists.

In a Phase 2 trial, participants on the highest dose achieved a mean 24.2% body-weight reduction at 48 weeks, the largest reported for any incretin agent to date. Phase 3 programs (TRIUMPH) are ongoing across obesity, type-2 diabetes, and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis.

Retatrutide's defining feature is balanced agonism across three incretin receptors. Unlike tirzepatide (dual GIP/GLP-1), the added glucagon-receptor activity drives hepatic lipid mobilization and raises basal energy expenditure, which appears to account for its outsized weight-loss magnitude.

GLP-1Slows gastric emptying, enhances glucose-dependent insulin secretion, central appetite suppression.
GIPImproves insulin sensitivity and modulates adipose lipid handling.
GlucagonIncreases resting energy expenditure and hepatic fat oxidation.
