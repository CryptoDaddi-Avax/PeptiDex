Retatrutide vs Semaglutide

Peptidex Editorial Team — independent research, no vendor sponsorship·Updated May 29, 2026·Sources: 24+142 indexed studies

Quick answer

Retatrutide is a triple-agonist with the largest reported Phase 2 weight reduction, while Semaglutide is a single GLP-1 agonist with by far the deepest clinical evidence base and FDA approval.

  • Retatrutide: 24.2% body-weight reduction at 48 weeks (Jastreboff et al., NEJM 2023).
  • Semaglutide: 14.9% body-weight loss at 68 weeks (STEP-1, NEJM 2021).
  • Retatrutide half-life is 168 h vs Semaglutide at 168 h; both administered SubQ.
  • Lowest per-mg pricing in the PeptiDex index: Retatrutide at $8.72/mg.

What's the difference between Retatrutide and Semaglutide?

Retatrutide is a triple-agonist with the largest reported Phase 2 weight reduction, while Semaglutide is a single GLP-1 agonist with by far the deepest clinical evidence base and FDA approval.

Which is more studied for fat loss, Retatrutide or Semaglutide?

Semaglutide carries more indexed studies (142 vs 24) in the PeptiDex library. Retatrutide is a glp-1/gip/glucagon triple-agonist; Semaglutide is a glp-1 agonist. Compare the full citation trails on each profile.

Can Retatrutide and Semaglutide be stacked?

They are not combined — both act on the GLP-1 receptor, so co-administration is redundant and raises GI side effects.
