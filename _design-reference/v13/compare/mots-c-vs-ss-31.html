MOTS-c vs SS-31

Peptidex Editorial Team — independent research, no vendor sponsorship·Updated May 29, 2026·Sources: 18+24 indexed studies

Quick answer

MOTS-c and SS-31 are both mitochondrial peptides with different targets: MOTS-c activates AMPK and metabolic flexibility, while SS-31 stabilizes cardiolipin to protect the electron-transport chain.

  • MOTS-c: Improved insulin sensitivity and exercise capacity in aged mice (Lee et al., Cell Metab 2015).
  • SS-31: Improved mitochondrial function in heart-failure patients (Daubert et al., JACC HF 2017).
  • MOTS-c half-life is ~5 h vs SS-31 at ~2 h; both administered SubQ.
  • Lowest per-mg pricing in the PeptiDex index: MOTS-c at $4.20/mg.

What's the difference between MOTS-c and SS-31?

MOTS-c and SS-31 are both mitochondrial peptides with different targets: MOTS-c activates AMPK and metabolic flexibility, while SS-31 stabilizes cardiolipin to protect the electron-transport chain.

Which is more studied for longevity, MOTS-c or SS-31?

SS-31 carries more indexed studies (24 vs 18) in the PeptiDex library. MOTS-c is a mitochondrial-derived peptide; SS-31 is a cardiolipin-targeted tetrapeptide. Compare the full citation trails on each profile.

Can MOTS-c and SS-31 be stacked?

Yes — MOTS-c and SS-31 are frequently co-studied in longevity contexts because their mechanisms (metabolic signaling vs. structural protection) are complementary.
