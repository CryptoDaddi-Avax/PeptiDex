What it does

Hexarelin is a synthetic hexapeptide GH secretagogue and the most potent of the standard GHRPs. Beyond GH release it binds the scavenger receptor CD36, which underlies its documented cardioprotective signaling.

Its dual GHSR/CD36 activity makes it of interest in both GH-axis and cardiovascular research, though tolerance to its GH-releasing effect can develop with continuous use.

Why researchers study it

  • Most potent GH release among standard GHRPs
  • Documented cardioprotective signaling via CD36
  • Dual GHSR-1a and CD36 receptor activity
  • Longer half-life than other hexapeptide GHRPs

Mechanism

Hexarelin strongly activates the GHSR-1a receptor to drive a potent pituitary GH pulse, while also binding the CD36 scavenger receptor on cardiac and vascular tissue.

The CD36 interaction mediates protective effects in ischemia-reperfusion models that are independent of GH, broadening its research interest beyond the somatotrope axis.

Potent GH release

Strong GHSR-1a activation yields the largest GH pulse among standard GHRPs.

Cardioprotection

CD36 binding mediates protective effects in ischemia-reperfusion models.

IGF-1 elevation

Increased GH output raises downstream IGF-1.
