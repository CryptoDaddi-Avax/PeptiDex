What it does

CJC-1295 is a modified analog of growth-hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH). It exists in two forms: a no-DAC variant with a short half-life, and a DAC (drug-affinity complex) variant that binds albumin to extend its action over several days.

By stimulating the pituitary GHRH receptor, it raises the amplitude of natural GH pulses. It is most often studied paired with a ghrelin mimetic such as Ipamorelin to engage two complementary GH-release pathways.

Why researchers study it

  • DAC variant sustains GH elevation across multiple days
  • Stimulates endogenous, pulsatile GH rather than exogenous GH
  • Synergizes with ghrelin mimetics like Ipamorelin
  • Well-characterized pituitary GHRH-receptor mechanism

Mechanism

CJC-1295 activates the pituitary GHRH receptor, increasing the amplitude of endogenous growth-hormone pulses. The DAC modification binds circulating albumin, protecting the peptide from rapid degradation.

Because it amplifies the body's own pulsatile release rather than supplying GH directly, it preserves the physiological rhythm of secretion — and pairs additively with ghrelin-receptor agonists.

GH pulse amplification

Activates the pituitary GHRH receptor to raise the amplitude of natural GH pulses.

Extended action (DAC)

Albumin binding via the drug-affinity complex sustains exposure over several days.

IGF-1 elevation

Increased GH output raises hepatic IGF-1, the principal downstream mediator.
