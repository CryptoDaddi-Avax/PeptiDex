What it does

Epitalon (epithalon) is a synthetic tetrapeptide (Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly) based on epithalamin, a polypeptide of the pineal gland. It was developed in Russia within the peptide-bioregulator research program.

Its most-cited property is the induction of telomerase activity in human somatic cells, alongside normalization of melatonin rhythms — placing it at the center of longevity-oriented peptide research.

Why researchers study it

  • Reactivated telomerase in human somatic cell studies
  • Normalizes melatonin and circadian rhythms
  • Associated with extended lifespan in animal cohorts
  • Administered in short, cyclical courses

Mechanism

Epitalon interacts with promoter regions of DNA to upregulate the telomerase reverse-transcriptase gene, transiently restoring telomerase activity that lengthens or maintains telomeres in dividing cells.

It also helps normalize pineal melatonin output and circadian signaling, contributing to the neuroendocrine-regulation effects reported in long-term cohort studies.
