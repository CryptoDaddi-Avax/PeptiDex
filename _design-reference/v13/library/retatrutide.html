What it does

Retatrutide simultaneously activates three incretin and metabolic receptors that the body normally engages separately. By combining GLP-1 and GIP agonism with glucagon receptor activation, it suppresses appetite, improves insulin handling, and raises energy expenditure at the same time — a mechanism no single-target agent reproduces.

In published Phase 2 work, once-weekly subcutaneous dosing produced dose-dependent weight reduction that exceeded results seen with dual GIP/GLP-1 agonists, alongside favorable effects on glycemic control and hepatic fat.

Mechanism

Retatrutide is a single peptide engineered to bind three distinct receptors with balanced potency. GLP-1 and GIP receptor agonism drive the incretin effect — enhancing glucose-dependent insulin secretion and slowing gastric emptying — while glucagon receptor agonism increases resting energy expenditure and hepatic fat mobilization.

The combination is synergistic: glucagon's catabolic, energy-burning effect offsets the appetite-driven calorie reduction from the incretin arms, producing weight loss through both reduced intake and increased output.

Appetite suppression

GLP-1 receptor activation acts on hypothalamic satiety centers and slows gastric emptying, reducing caloric intake.

Insulin sensitivity

GIP agonism enhances glucose-dependent insulin secretion and improves peripheral insulin sensitivity.

Energy expenditure

Glucagon receptor activation raises resting metabolic rate and mobilizes hepatic fat stores.
