What it does

Semaglutide is a long-acting GLP-1 receptor agonist engineered for once-weekly subcutaneous dosing. It mimics the incretin hormone GLP-1, enhancing glucose-dependent insulin secretion and acting centrally to reduce appetite.

It is the most extensively studied agent in its class, with a large body of cardiovascular-outcome and weight-management evidence from the SUSTAIN, STEP, and SELECT trial programs.

Mechanism

Semaglutide selectively activates the GLP-1 receptor, amplifying glucose-dependent insulin release from pancreatic beta cells and suppressing inappropriate glucagon secretion.

Centrally, it engages hypothalamic appetite circuits and slows gastric emptying, reducing caloric intake — the combination responsible for its glycemic and weight effects.

Appetite suppression

Acts on hypothalamic satiety centers and slows gastric emptying, reducing caloric intake.

Glycemic control

Enhances glucose-dependent insulin secretion and suppresses excess glucagon.

Cardiometabolic effects

Associated with improved cardiovascular outcomes in high-risk populations (SELECT).
