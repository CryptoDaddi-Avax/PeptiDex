What it does

DSIP (delta sleep-inducing peptide) is a naturally occurring nonapeptide first isolated from the brains of sleeping rabbits. It is named for its ability to promote delta-wave (slow-wave) sleep.

Beyond sleep, it has been studied for stress resilience, analgesia, and normalization of disturbed circadian and neuroendocrine rhythms.

Mechanism

DSIP modulates several neurotransmitter and neuroendocrine systems — interacting with GABAergic tone, opioid/endorphin signaling, and the regulation of corticosteroid and melatonin rhythms.

These combined effects shift sleep architecture toward slow-wave sleep and dampen stress-axis activity, although its precise receptor target remains incompletely defined.
