Compounds researched for appetite control and lipolysis — from the most-studied GLP-1 agonist to targeted GH-fragment and monoamine agents.

What peptides are most studied for fat loss?

The most-studied fat-loss peptide is Semaglutide, the GLP-1 agonist with the deepest clinical evidence base. It is supported in research by the GH-fragment lipolytic AOD-9604 and the oral monoamine agent Tesofensine, which add direct fat-breakdown and energy-expenditure mechanisms.

How do these peptides work for fat loss?

These three approach fat loss from complementary directions:

  • Semaglutide. Acts on hypothalamic satiety centers and slows gastric emptying, reducing caloric intake — the best-evidenced appetite mechanism in the class.
  • AOD-9604. Stimulates lipolysis and inhibits lipogenesis without raising IGF-1 or blood glucose — a targeted fat-breakdown mechanism.
  • Tesofensine. Raises dopamine, norepinephrine, and serotonin tone, combining appetite suppression with increased energy expenditure.

Which research stacks target fat loss?

The Cutting Stack is the documented fat-loss combination, pairing appetite control with lipolytic support. The Recomp Stack is the alternative when preserving lean mass is a priority.

  • The Cutting Stack — Semaglutide + AOD-9604 + L-Carnitine over a clean 10-week research cycle.
  • The Recomp Stack — When lean-mass preservation matters as much as fat loss.
