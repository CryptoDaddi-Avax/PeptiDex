Peptides most studied for immune support

Compounds researched for immune resilience — T-cell maturation, broad antimicrobial activity, and inflammatory modulation.

Quick answer

Immune research is led by Thymosin Alpha-1 for T-cell maturation, the cathelicidin LL-37 for broad antimicrobial defense, and KPV for anti-inflammatory modulation — the most-studied immune-resilience combination.

  • Thymosin Alpha-1 improved survival in severe sepsis (Wu et al., Crit Care 2013, n=361).

What peptides are most studied for immune support?

The most-studied immune-support peptides are Thymosin Alpha-1 for T-cell maturation, LL-37 for antimicrobial defense, and KPV for anti-inflammatory balance. Thymosin Alpha-1 carries the largest clinical evidence base of the three.

Thymosin Alpha-1Immune peptide 28-aa peptide approved abroad as Zadaxin for immune modulation. LL-37Antimicrobial Human cathelicidin with broad-spectrum antimicrobial and immune-modulating roles. KPVAnti-inflammatory Alpha-MSH tripeptide fragment balancing immune over-activation.

How do these peptides work for immune support?

They cover three arms of immune function:

  • Thymosin Alpha-1. Promotes T-cell maturation through Toll-like-receptor signaling, restoring balanced immune function in compromised states.
  • LL-37. Disrupts microbial membranes while recruiting immune cells, acting as a frontline innate-immunity peptide.
  • KPV. Lowers TNF-α and IL-6 output via NF-κB inhibition, dampening the inflammatory side of immune dysregulation.
