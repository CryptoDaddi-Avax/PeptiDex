Peptides most studied for gut health

Compounds researched for the gut barrier — mucosal repair, inflammatory modulation, and tight-junction integrity.

Quick answer

Gut-health research centers on BPC-157 for mucosal repair, KPV for intestinal anti-inflammation, and Larazotide for tight-junction integrity — covering structural repair, inflammation, and permeability.

  • BPC-157 is studied for protective and reparative effects on the gastrointestinal lining.
  • KPV reduced colitis severity via NF-κB inhibition (Inflamm Bowel Dis 2008).
  • Larazotide reduced symptoms in celiac disease Phase 2b (Leffler et al., 2015).
  • The Healing Stack supports tissue remodeling over an 8-week research cycle.

What peptides are most studied for gut health?

The most-studied gut-health peptides are BPC-157 for mucosal repair, KPV for intestinal anti-inflammation, and Larazotide for tight-junction integrity. Larazotide is the most clinically advanced, having reached Phase 3 for celiac disease.

BPC-157Pentadecapeptide Gastric-derived peptide studied extensively for gut-lining repair. KPVAnti-inflammatory Alpha-MSH fragment with strong intestinal anti-inflammatory data. LarazotideZonulin antagonist Octapeptide tight-junction regulator and the most clinically advanced of the group.

How do these peptides work for gut health?

They target the gut barrier at three levels:

  • BPC-157. Drives angiogenesis and cytoprotective signaling in the gut wall, the structural-repair mechanism for mucosal research.
  • KPV. Inhibits NF-κB in intestinal epithelium, lowering the cytokine signaling that drives gut inflammation.
  • Larazotide. Antagonizes zonulin to keep intestinal tight junctions closed, reducing paracellular permeability ('leaky gut').
