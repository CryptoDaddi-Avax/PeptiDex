§ GLOSSARY

Peptide research glossary

70+ terms from peptide pharmacology, lab methodology, and vendor verification — each defined in one plain sentence.

Peptide #

A peptide is a short chain of amino acids linked by peptide bonds, typically fewer than 50 residues. Research peptides are studied for their signaling activity at specific receptors.

Reconstitution #

Reconstitution is the process of dissolving a lyophilized (freeze-dried) peptide into bacteriostatic water to create an injectable solution. The volume of BAC water used determines the final concentration.

Bacteriostatic Water (BAC) #

Bacteriostatic water is sterile water containing 0.9% benzyl alcohol as a preservative. It is the standard diluent for multi-dose peptide vials, allowing reconstituted peptides to be stored for up to 28 days under refrigeration.

Lyophilization #

Lyophilization is freeze-drying — removing water from a peptide under vacuum to produce a stable powder that must be reconstituted before use.

HPLC #

HPLC (High-Performance Liquid Chromatography) is an analytical technique used to verify peptide purity. A Certificate of Analysis should include HPLC results showing percent purity, typically 98% or higher for research-grade peptides.

Mass Spectrometry #

Mass spectrometry is an analytical technique that confirms a peptide's molecular weight and identity by measuring the mass-to-charge ratio of its ions. It complements HPLC on a Certificate of Analysis.

Certificate of Analysis (COA) #

A Certificate of Analysis is a vendor-supplied document reporting a peptide batch's measured purity (HPLC) and identity (mass spec). COA transparency is a primary signal of vendor reliability.

Purity #

Purity is the percentage of a sample that is the intended peptide, measured by HPLC. Research-grade peptides typically report 98–99%+ purity.

Half-life #

Half-life (t½) is the time required for the plasma concentration of a compound to fall by half. It determines dosing frequency — longer half-lives support less frequent administration.

Pharmacokinetics (PK) #

Pharmacokinetics describes how a compound is absorbed, distributed, metabolized, and eliminated over time, often visualized as a plasma-concentration curve.

Subcutaneous (SubQ) #

Subcutaneous administration delivers a compound into the fatty layer beneath the skin using a short, fine needle. It is the most common route in peptide research protocols.

Intramuscular (IM) #

Intramuscular administration delivers a compound directly into muscle tissue, producing different absorption kinetics than subcutaneous injection.

Intranasal #

Intranasal administration delivers a peptide across the nasal mucosa, used for several neuropeptides such as Semax and Selank to reach the central nervous system.

Insulin Syringe (U-100) #

A U-100 insulin syringe is graduated so that 100 units equal 1 mL. It is the standard tool for measuring small subcutaneous peptide doses.

Titration #

Titration is the gradual stepwise increase of a dose over time, used to improve tolerability — common with incretin agonists that cause initial nausea.

Loading Dose #

A loading dose is a larger initial dose given to reach an effective concentration quickly before transitioning to a lower maintenance dose.

Maintenance Dose #

A maintenance dose is the ongoing dose used to sustain a steady plasma concentration after initiation or loading.

Cycle #

A cycle is a defined period of administration followed by a break, used in research protocols to model intermittent exposure.

GLP-1 #

GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) is an incretin hormone that enhances glucose-dependent insulin secretion and reduces appetite. GLP-1 receptor agonists are studied for weight and glycemic outcomes.

GIP #

GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide) is an incretin hormone that enhances insulin secretion and insulin sensitivity; it is targeted alongside GLP-1 by dual agonists such as tirzepatide.

Glucagon Receptor #

The glucagon receptor mediates increased energy expenditure and hepatic fat mobilization; it is the third target of triple agonists such as retatrutide.

Incretin #

An incretin is a gut hormone (GLP-1 or GIP) released after eating that stimulates insulin secretion. Incretin-based agonists are the foundation of modern weight-management research.

Triple Agonist #

A triple agonist is a single peptide that activates three receptors at once — for retatrutide, the GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon receptors.

GHRH #

GHRH (growth-hormone-releasing hormone) stimulates the pituitary to release growth hormone. GHRH analogs such as CJC-1295, sermorelin, and tesamorelin amplify natural GH pulses.

GHRP #

A GHRP (growth-hormone-releasing peptide) is a ghrelin-receptor agonist that triggers a pulse of growth hormone — examples include ipamorelin, GHRP-2, GHRP-6, and hexarelin.

Ghrelin Receptor (GHSR) #

The growth-hormone secretagogue receptor (GHSR-1a) is the target of ghrelin and synthetic GHRPs, driving pulsatile GH release.

Secretagogue #

A secretagogue is a compound that stimulates the secretion of another substance — GH secretagogues prompt the body to release its own growth hormone.

IGF-1 #

IGF-1 (insulin-like growth factor 1) is the principal downstream mediator of growth hormone, produced mainly in the liver, that drives tissue growth and repair.

Myostatin #

Myostatin (GDF-8) is the chief negative regulator of muscle mass. Inhibiting it — as follistatin does — releases the brake on muscle growth.

Angiogenesis #

Angiogenesis is the formation of new blood vessels. Peptides such as BPC-157 promote angiogenesis to support tissue repair.

Pulsatile Release #

Pulsatile release is the natural pattern of hormone secretion in discrete bursts. GHRH analogs and GnRH peptides preserve this rhythm, unlike continuous exogenous hormone.

Melanocortin Receptor #

Melanocortin receptors (MC1R–MC5R) mediate effects from skin pigmentation (MC1R) to sexual arousal (MC4R). PT-141 and Melanotan II are melanocortin agonists.

Telomerase #

Telomerase is the enzyme that maintains telomeres, the protective caps on chromosomes. Epitalon is studied for transient telomerase induction.

Senolytic #

A senolytic is a compound that selectively triggers death in senescent ('zombie') cells. FOXO4-DRI is a peptide senolytic studied for tissue rejuvenation.

Cellular Senescence #

Cellular senescence is a state in which cells stop dividing but resist death, secreting inflammatory factors that accumulate with age.

NAD+ #

NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a coenzyme essential to energy metabolism and DNA repair, and the substrate for sirtuin enzymes. Its levels decline with age.

Sirtuins #

Sirtuins are NAD+-dependent enzymes that regulate stress resistance, metabolism, and aging by deacetylating target proteins.

Mitochondrial-Derived Peptide (MDP) #

An MDP is a peptide encoded within mitochondrial DNA — examples are MOTS-c and humanin — studied for metabolic and cytoprotective signaling.

Cardiolipin #

Cardiolipin is a phospholipid of the inner mitochondrial membrane essential to the electron-transport chain; SS-31 binds and stabilizes it.

BDNF #

BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) supports neuron survival and synaptic plasticity. Nootropic peptides such as Semax and Selank upregulate it.

NGF #

NGF (nerve growth factor) supports the growth and maintenance of neurons; it is upregulated by peptides such as Semax.

Nootropic #

A nootropic is a compound studied for cognitive enhancement — improving memory, focus, or neuroprotection.

Anxiolytic #

An anxiolytic reduces anxiety. Selank is studied as an anxiolytic that does not cause the sedation or dependence associated with benzodiazepines.

GABA #

GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid) is the principal inhibitory neurotransmitter; anxiolytic peptides modulate GABAergic tone.

Synaptogenesis #

Synaptogenesis is the formation of new synapses between neurons. Dihexa is studied for potent synaptogenesis via the HGF/c-Met pathway.

TREK-1 #

TREK-1 is a potassium channel that regulates mood and neuronal excitability; blocking it (as PE-22-28 does) is linked to rapid antidepressant effects.

HPG Axis #

The hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis governs reproductive hormones. Gonadorelin and kisspeptin act at successive points along this axis.

GnRH #

GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone) drives pituitary release of LH and FSH. Gonadorelin is synthetic GnRH; kisspeptin acts one step upstream.

Kisspeptin #

Kisspeptin is the neuropeptide at the top of the reproductive cascade, stimulating GnRH neurons via the KISS1 receptor.

LH / FSH #

Luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) are pituitary gonadotropins that drive gonadal steroidogenesis and fertility.

Zonulin #

Zonulin is the protein that loosens intestinal tight junctions, increasing gut permeability. Larazotide antagonizes it to support barrier integrity.

Tight Junctions #

Tight junctions are protein complexes sealing the space between intestinal epithelial cells; their integrity governs gut-barrier function.

Cathelicidin #

Cathelicidin (LL-37) is the only human cathelicidin antimicrobial peptide, providing innate immune defense and immune modulation.

Antimicrobial Peptide #

An antimicrobial peptide disrupts microbial membranes as part of innate immunity; LL-37 and KPV have antimicrobial activity.

Thymosin #

Thymosins are thymus-derived peptides; Thymosin Alpha-1 modulates immunity and Thymosin Beta-4 (TB-500) regulates tissue repair.

Lipolysis #

Lipolysis is the breakdown of stored fat into free fatty acids. AOD-9604 is studied for lipolytic activity without growth-hormone side effects.

NNMT #

NNMT (nicotinamide N-methyltransferase) is an enzyme overexpressed in obese adipose tissue; inhibiting it (5-Amino-1MQ) preserves NAD+ and shifts metabolism.

Amylin #

Amylin is a pancreatic hormone co-secreted with insulin that signals satiety; cagrilintide is a long-acting amylin analog.

mTOR #

mTOR is a central signaling pathway that drives protein synthesis and cell growth, activated downstream of IGF-1 and IGF-1 LR3.

VEGFR2 #

VEGFR2 is a vascular endothelial growth factor receptor central to angiogenesis; BPC-157 upregulates VEGFR2 signaling.

Lipohypertrophy #

Lipohypertrophy is the buildup of fatty lumps at repeatedly used injection sites; rotating sites helps prevent it.

Aspiration #

Aspiration is briefly pulling back the syringe plunger before injecting to check for blood, indicating the needle is not in a vessel.

Sharps Container #

A sharps container is an FDA-cleared puncture-resistant bin for safe disposal of used needles and syringes.

MASH #

MASH (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis) is a liver condition studied as an endpoint for incretin agents such as retatrutide.

Phase 2 / Phase 3 #

Phase 2 and Phase 3 are stages of clinical trials testing efficacy and safety in progressively larger participant groups.

MACE #

MACE (major adverse cardiovascular events) is a composite trial endpoint used to assess cardiovascular safety.

PEPTIDEX Code #

PEPTIDEX is the discount code applied at participating COA-verified vendors; per editorial policy, affiliate codes do not affect vendor rankings.

Research Use Only (RUO) #

Research Use Only means a compound is intended for laboratory study and is not approved by the FDA or other regulators for human use.

DOI #

A DOI (Digital Object Identifier) is a persistent link to a published study, used throughout Peptidex profiles to attribute every efficacy claim.

PubMed #

PubMed is the U.S. National Library of Medicine database of biomedical literature, a primary source for peptide study citations.
