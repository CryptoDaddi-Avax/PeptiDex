## How We Evaluate Vendors: Our 5-Point Framework

Our editorial team uses a standardized, independently-applied evaluation framework. No vendor can pay for placement or influence our ratings. Here is exactly what we measure:

### 1. COA Transparency and Verification
Does the vendor publish batch-specific COAs for every product? Are they from a recognized independent lab? Can they be verified by calling the laboratory? If a vendor refuses to share a COA before purchase, they automatically fail our evaluation.

### 2. HPLC Purity Standards
High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC) purity must meet a ≥98% threshold to be considered viable for serious research. Mass spectrometry must confirm the correct molecular weight. We independently verify these claims through blind testing.

### 3. Third-Party Lab Independence
Testing must come from an independent analytical lab — not the vendor's own internal facility. An internal COA is a massive conflict of interest. We prioritize vendors who use Janoshik Analytical, MZ Biolabs, and comparable standards.

### 4. Shipping Reliability
Domestic US delivery must consistently land under 5 business days. The vendor must provide tracking and utilize appropriate packaging for heat-sensitive compounds. We evaluate their reshipment policies for damaged or lost packages.

### 5. Community Reputation
We monitor independent research forums, community boards, and verified review platforms (like Trustpilot) for consistent, positive researcher feedback. Vendors with a pattern of "bait-and-switch" tactics or unresponsiveness are blacklisted.

## Red Flags: Spotting Unreliable Vendors

Not every vendor that looks professional is trustworthy. The proliferation of Shopify templates means anyone can set up a "chemical supply company" in an afternoon. Here are the warning signs our team watches for:

- **No COA available:** Or COAs that lack independent lab identification.
- **Suspiciously low prices:** If a compound is 50%+ cheaper than every major competitor, the purity is compromised. Quality synthesis and independent testing are expensive.
- **Round purity numbers:** Real analytical testing produces decimal values (e.g., 99.4%). COAs showing exactly "99.00%" are often forged.
- **No batch/lot numbers:** If a certificate lacks a lot number, traceability is impossible.
- **Medical Marketing:** Vendors selling "stacks for bodybuilding" or providing human dosing protocols are actively violating FDA marketing regulations for research chemicals, indicating a fundamentally unstable business model.
