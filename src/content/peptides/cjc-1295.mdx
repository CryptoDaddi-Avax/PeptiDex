## What is CJC-1295?

CJC-1295 is a 30-amino-acid GHRH analog that incorporates four amino-acid
substitutions (replacing labile residues) plus a Drug Affinity Complex (DAC)
maleimidopropionic acid moiety that covalently binds circulating serum albumin —
extending its plasma half-life from the few minutes of native GHRH to roughly a
week [1]. The DAC-free variant of the modified GHRH 1-29 sequence is sometimes
referred to as "Modified GRF 1-29" or "CJC-1295 without DAC" in research-community
literature; on PeptiDex this profile primarily refers to the DAC variant unless
otherwise noted.

CJC-1295 is **not approved by the FDA** for any therapeutic indication and is
classified as a research compound. WADA-related anti-doping detection methods for
CJC-1295 have been validated in published assays [4].

## How CJC-1295 Works (Mechanism of Action)

### Receptor binding and pathway

CJC-1295 binds the GHRH receptor on anterior-pituitary somatotrophs and stimulates
the release of stored growth hormone. Because the DAC modification produces a
sustained plasma exposure, the agent provides a multi-day stimulus to GH release —
but the published clinical work (n=21 healthy adults) showed that the **pulsatile
pattern of endogenous GH secretion is preserved**, rather than being replaced by
flat hyper-secretion [1][2].

The downstream effect is elevation of GH and, secondarily, IGF-1 from hepatic
secretion in response to GH binding hepatic GH receptors [1].

## Clinical & Preclinical Evidence

### Human studies

| Study (year) | Population | n | Dose | Duration | Key outcome | Citation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Teichman et al. (2006) | Healthy adult volunteers | 21 (pooled cohorts) | 30 / 60 / 125 µg/kg single SC dose | observation up to 14 days | Mean GH increased multi-fold for 6+ days; IGF-1 elevated for 9–11 days | [1] |
| Ionescu & Frohman (2006) | Healthy adults | n smaller | repeated SC dosing | weeks | 7.5-fold increase in basal GH; 46% increase in mean GH; 45% increase in IGF-1 — pulsatile pattern preserved | [2] |

The published clinical evidence base for CJC-1295 is small — two principal human
studies in healthy volunteers — and there is no Phase 3 program in any
indication.

### Animal and preclinical studies

> Findings from animal models may not translate to human outcomes.

Animal data indicate that long-acting GHRH analogs upregulate total pituitary RNA
and somatotroph GH mRNA, suggesting the prolonged exposure may also influence
somatotroph proliferation as well as acute GH release [3].

## Dosing in Research Settings

> The numbers below describe dosing **as observed in published research and as
> commonly discussed in research-community practice** — not recommendations.

### Reported dosing

The Teichman et al. clinical study used single subcutaneous doses of 30, 60, and
125 µg/kg in healthy adults [1]. Research-community practice commonly discusses
weekly dosing of approximately 1–2 mg per administration for the DAC variant; the
DAC-free variant ("Modified GRF 1-29") is typically discussed at smaller, more
frequent doses because of its much shorter half-life.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1. What is CJC-1295?**
CJC-1295 is a 30-amino-acid GHRH analog with a Drug Affinity Complex (DAC)
modification that binds plasma albumin, extending its half-life from minutes
(native GHRH) to days [1]. It is not FDA-approved.

**Q2. What is the half-life of CJC-1295?**
The DAC variant's reported subcutaneous half-life is approximately 6–8 days,
consistent with the multi-day GH/IGF-1 elevation observed after a single
injection in healthy volunteers [1]. The DAC-free variant ("Modified GRF 1-29")
has a much shorter half-life and is dosed more frequently.

**Q3. CJC-1295 vs Ipamorelin — what's the research difference?**
CJC-1295 acts on the GHRH receptor; Ipamorelin acts on the GHS-R1a (ghrelin)
receptor. They are mechanistically complementary, which is why the combination
is widely discussed. No controlled head-to-head or combination trial has been
published. See [`CJC-1295 vs Ipamorelin`](/vs/cjc-1295-vs-ipamorelin).

**Q4. Does CJC-1295 affect IGF-1 levels?**
Yes — in the published clinical study, single doses produced IGF-1 elevations
above baseline for 9–11 days [1]. Repeated dosing produced a sustained ~45%
increase in IGF-1 vs baseline [2].

**Q5. Is CJC-1295 detectable in drug tests?**
Yes. Multiple published assays validate detection of CJC-1295 in urine and blood
at low pg/mL concentrations using LC-MS/MS, immuno-PCR, and antibody-free
methods [4]. CJC-1295 is included in WADA's prohibited peptide-hormone category.
{/* TODO: Cite WADA list section. */}

**Q6. What were the doses used in the Teichman et al. study?**
Single subcutaneous doses of 30, 60, and 125 µg/kg in healthy adult volunteers,
with observations up to 14 days post-injection [1].

**Q7. CJC-1295 with DAC vs without DAC — what's the difference?**
The DAC modification covalently links the peptide to circulating serum albumin,
extending half-life from minutes to days. The DAC-free version ("Modified GRF
1-29") has a much shorter half-life and is dosed more frequently in research-
community practice. The published clinical study [1] used the DAC variant.

**Q8. Does CJC-1295 increase appetite?**
Increased appetite has been reported in research-community pharmacovigilance, but
is not a documented finding in the small published clinical literature [1][2].

**Q9. Does CJC-1295 affect blood sugar?**
GH elevation can increase insulin resistance over time. The published clinical
literature did not focus on glucose endpoints [1][2]. Diabetic patients should
take this theoretical concern seriously when discussing GH secretagogues with a
clinician.

**Q10. How long do CJC-1295 cycles last in research?**
Published clinical studies dosed for shorter observation windows (single dose to
multi-week) [1][2]. Research-community cycles vary widely and are not based on
controlled-trial protocols.

**Q11. Can CJC-1295 be combined with semaglutide?**
The on-site dataset records this combination as cautioned because GLP-1 agonism
may blunt GH-secretagogue effects in some animal data. No published human study
has assessed the combination directly.

**Q12. Is CJC-1295 the same as sermorelin?**
No. Both are GHRH analogs but sermorelin (GHRH 1-29) has a half-life measured in
minutes, requiring frequent dosing; CJC-1295 (especially the DAC variant) was
designed to provide a multi-day half-life [1].
