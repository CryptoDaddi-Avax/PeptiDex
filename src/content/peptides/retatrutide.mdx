## What is Retatrutide?

Retatrutide (development code **LY3437943**) is an investigational once-weekly
synthetic peptide engineered by Eli Lilly to activate three incretin and
counter-regulatory receptors simultaneously: GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon. The dual
GLP-1/GIP combination was first commercialised as tirzepatide; retatrutide adds
glucagon-receptor agonism to that scaffold, with the goal of further increasing
energy expenditure and hepatic lipid oxidation [4].

Retatrutide is **not approved by the FDA** as of {frontmatter.reviewedDate}. The
Phase 3 TRIUMPH program (multiple parallel trials covering obesity, T2D, OSA, and
metabolic-associated steatohepatitis) is ongoing.

---

## How Retatrutide Works (Mechanism of Action)

### Receptor pharmacology

Retatrutide is a balanced agonist at three receptors [4]:

- **GLP-1 receptor** — appetite suppression, glucose-dependent insulin secretion,
  delayed gastric emptying.
- **GIP receptor** — additional insulinotropic action; effects on adipose tissue
  metabolism.
- **Glucagon receptor** — increased energy expenditure, hepatic lipid oxidation,
  potential offset of the weight-gain effect that historically limited
  glucagon-only agents.

The triple-agonism rationale is that adding glucagon-receptor activation to the
GLP-1/GIP combination should further increase energy expenditure beyond what
GLP-1- or dual-agonist therapies achieve alone — at the cost of needing to
balance glucagon's hyperglycemic effect against GLP-1/GIP insulinotropic action.

## Clinical & Preclinical Evidence

### Human studies

| Study (year) | Population | n | Dose | Duration | Key outcome | Citation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jastreboff et al. Phase 2 (2023) | Adults with obesity, no T2D | 338 | 1 / 4 / 8 / 12 mg SC weekly | 48 wk | −24.2% body weight at 12 mg vs −2.1% placebo | [1] |
| Coskun et al. Phase 1 (2022) | Healthy adults / T2D | dose-escalation | up to 12 mg SC | up to 12 wk | Dose-dependent weight loss; HbA1c reductions; safety profile suitable for Phase 2 | [2] |
| Sanyal et al. Phase 2a MASLD (2024) | Adults with MASLD | smaller substudy | retatrutide doses | up to 48 wk | Liver-fat reduction; high rates of steatosis resolution | [3] |

> **Caveat about apparent magnitude.** The 24.2% Phase 2 result has been compared
> in popular press to bariatric-surgery effect sizes. Phase 2 trials are smaller
> and shorter than Phase 3, and Phase 3 effect sizes are commonly somewhat
> smaller. The TRIUMPH Phase 3 program will produce the data on which any
> regulatory decision is made.

### Animal and preclinical studies

The triple-agonism design is supported by mechanistic studies in rodent obesity
models [4]. Clinical Phase 2 data is now far more relevant for clinical
decision-making than the animal characterisation data.

## Dosing in Research Settings

> Retatrutide is **investigational**. The doses below describe schedules used in
> the published Phase 2 program — they are not a recommendation. There is no FDA
> label.

### Reported dosing — Phase 2 [1]

Retatrutide was titrated in 4-week increments starting at 2 mg, with maintenance
arms at 1, 4, 8, and 12 mg once weekly subcutaneously. Trial titration in the
Jastreboff et al. publication [1]:

| Weeks 1–4 | Weeks 5–8 | Weeks 9–12 | Weeks 13–16 | Maintenance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 mg | 4 mg | 6 mg | 8 mg | up to 12 mg |

Different dose arms followed shorter or longer titration depending on the target
maintenance dose [1].

### Half-life and timing

The reported half-life is consistent with once-weekly dosing on the same day
each week.

### Reconstitution

Retatrutide for clinical-trial use is supplied as either a pre-filled pen or as
a vial requiring reconstitution depending on the trial site. For research-only
laboratory use, vial reconstitution requires the
[reconstitution calculator](/tools/calculator).

## Safety, Side Effects & Contraindications

### Reported adverse events

From Jastreboff et al. Phase 2 [1] and Coskun et al. Phase 1 [2]: GI events
predominate, similar to GLP-1 and GLP-1/GIP agents. The 12 mg arm in Phase 2
showed higher rates of nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea than lower-dose arms.

### Populations who should avoid

Phase 2 exclusion criteria included history of medullary thyroid carcinoma,
MEN 2, severe GI disease, history of pancreatitis, and pregnancy [1]. These
exclusions are conservative and likely to be reflected in any future label.

### Drug interactions

No formal drug-interaction studies have been published. Concomitant insulin or
sulfonylureas should be expected to require dose reduction, as for other
incretin therapies.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1. What is retatrutide?**
Retatrutide (LY3437943) is an investigational once-weekly synthetic peptide that
activates GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon receptors. In a 2023 Phase 2 trial, the 12 mg
dose produced ≈ 24.2% body-weight reduction at 48 weeks in adults with obesity
[1]. It is not FDA-approved.

**Q2. Is retatrutide FDA-approved?**
No. Retatrutide is in Phase 3 development (the TRIUMPH program). There is no FDA
label and no approved indication.

**Q3. How does retatrutide compare to tirzepatide and semaglutide?**
Retatrutide adds glucagon-receptor agonism to the GLP-1/GIP scaffold of
tirzepatide. Phase 2 retatrutide produced ≈ 24.2% at 48 weeks [1]; Phase 3
tirzepatide produced ≈ 22.5% at 72 weeks (SURMOUNT-1); Phase 3 semaglutide
produced ≈ 14.9% at 68 weeks (STEP 1). Phase-2-vs-Phase-3 comparisons should be
read with caution.

**Q6. Can retatrutide be combined with semaglutide or tirzepatide?**

**Q7. Does retatrutide affect liver fat?**

the dedicated Phase 3 trials in the TRIUMPH-MASH track.

**Q9. How is retatrutide dosed in trials?**
Phase 2 used 4-week titration starting at 2 mg subcutaneously, escalating to

**Q11. Is "research-grade" retatrutide the same as the trial drug?**
No. The trial drug is manufactured under cGMP for clinical use. Research-chemical
retatrutide has no FDA review, no manufacturing-quality guarantee, and is not
intended for human consumption.

**Q12. Does retatrutide cause heart-rate increase?**
A small but reportable mean heart-rate increase was observed in Phase 2 [1] —
consistent with effects seen with other incretin therapies. Long-term
cardiovascular significance, if any, will need Phase 3 outcome data.
