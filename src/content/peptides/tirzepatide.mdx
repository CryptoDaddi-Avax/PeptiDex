## What is Tirzepatide?

Tirzepatide is a 39-amino-acid synthetic peptide that activates **both** the
glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) receptor and the glucagon-like
peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor — the first FDA-approved dual incretin agonist. It is
sold under two brand names:

- **Mounjaro** — FDA-approved May 2022 for adults with type 2 diabetes [9].
- **Zepbound** — FDA-approved November 2023 for chronic weight management [10].

Tirzepatide is a **prescription medicine with a complete FDA labeling and a Phase 3
program (SURPASS for diabetes, SURMOUNT for obesity) covering tens of thousands of
participants** [1][3][4][5][6]. It is not a research-only compound.

---

## How Tirzepatide Works (Mechanism of Action)

### Receptor binding and pathway

Tirzepatide is engineered as a single peptide that activates both the GLP-1 and
GIP receptors. The molecular design includes a C20 fatty diacid moiety for albumin
binding and amino-acid substitutions that produce DPP-4 resistance — together
giving the once-weekly half-life of ≈ 120 hours [9].

The dual mechanism is hypothesised to produce additive metabolic effects:

- **GLP-1 agonism** — glucose-dependent insulin secretion, glucagon suppression,
  delayed gastric emptying, central appetite suppression.
- **GIP agonism** — additional insulin release and effects on adipose tissue
  metabolism and lipid handling that the GLP-1-only agents do not produce.

### Pharmacokinetics summary

| Property | Reported value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Half-life (subcutaneous) | ≈ 120 h (≈ 5 days) | [9] |
| Time to steady state | ~4 weeks | [9] |
| Bioavailability (subcutaneous) | ≈ 80% | [9] |
| Plasma protein binding (albumin) | ≈ 99% | [9] |
| Primary clearance | Proteolytic | [9] |

### Animal and preclinical studies

The molecular and receptor-pharmacology characterisation that supported the dual
GIP/GLP-1 design is summarised in published mechanistic reviews. Clinical Phase 3
data is now far more relevant for clinical decision-making.

## Dosing in Research Settings

> The dosing below is the FDA-approved label schedule. Tirzepatide is a
> prescription medicine; a licensed clinician directs initiation, titration, and
> maintenance.

### Reported dosing — Zepbound (chronic weight management) [10]

Initiate at 2.5 mg once weekly subcutaneously. Escalate every 4 weeks as tolerated:

| Week 1–4 | Week 5–8 | Week 9–12 | Week 13–16 | Week 17–20 | Week 21+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.5 mg | 5 mg | 7.5 mg | 10 mg | 12.5 mg | 15 mg |

Maintenance doses approved are 5 mg, 10 mg, and 15 mg.

### Reported dosing — Mounjaro (T2D) [9]

Same titration as Zepbound; same approved maintenance doses (5 / 10 / 15 mg).

### Half-life and timing

The ≈ 120-hour subcutaneous half-life [9] is the basis for once-weekly dosing on
the same day each week.

### Reconstitution

Tirzepatide is supplied as a single-use, ready-to-use pre-filled pen
(KwikPen / autoinjector) and **does not require reconstitution**. Compounded
tirzepatide is subject to the same FDA shortage-status considerations as
compounded semaglutide.

## Safety, Side Effects & Contraindications

### Reported adverse events

The Mounjaro and Zepbound labels report the following Phase 3 incidence (most
mild–moderate, transient during titration) [9][10]:

| Event | Frequency (vs placebo) | Severity | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nausea | 12–33% (dose-dependent) | mild–moderate | [9][10] |
| Diarrhea | 12–22% | mild–moderate | [9][10] |
| Vomiting | 5–13% | mild–moderate | [9][10] |
| Constipation | 6–11% | mild | [9][10] |
| Acute pancreatitis | uncommon, label warning | serious | [9] |
| Acute gallbladder disease | uncommon, label warning | moderate–serious | [10] |

### Populations who should avoid

The Mounjaro and Zepbound labels carry a **boxed warning** for thyroid C-cell
tumor risk based on rodent data; tirzepatide is contraindicated in patients with a
personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or MEN 2 [9][10].

Other label-listed cautions [9][10]:

- Pregnancy — discontinue when pregnancy is recognised; the long half-life means
  pre-conception planning is needed.
- History of pancreatitis.
- Severe gastrointestinal disease (e.g., gastroparesis).
- Combination with insulin or sulfonylureas — increases hypoglycemia risk;
  reduce secretagogue dose accordingly.

### Drug interactions

Slowed gastric emptying may affect absorption of concomitant oral medications,
particularly hormonal contraceptives and oral medications with narrow therapeutic
indices [9]. Patients on oral contraceptives are advised to switch to a non-oral
method or add a barrier method for 4 weeks after initiation and after each dose
escalation [10].

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1. What is tirzepatide?**
Tirzepatide is a long-acting dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist FDA-approved as
Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes (2022) and Zepbound for chronic weight management
(2023) [9][10]. In the Phase 3 SURMOUNT-1 trial, the 15 mg weekly dose produced
≈ 22.5% body weight reduction at 72 weeks [1].

**Q2. What is the half-life of tirzepatide?**
The subcutaneous half-life is approximately 120 hours, the basis for once-weekly
dosing [9]. Steady state is reached after about four weeks. The half-life is
shorter than semaglutide's (~168 h) but still long enough that pre-conception
planning is recommended.

**Q3. Tirzepatide vs Semaglutide — which produces more weight loss?**
In the SURPASS-2 head-to-head trial in T2D, tirzepatide produced greater HbA1c and
weight reduction than semaglutide 1 mg [4]. In Phase 3 obesity trials, the highest
tirzepatide dose (15 mg, SURMOUNT-1, ≈ 22.5%) [1] produced a larger mean weight
reduction than the highest semaglutide dose (2.4 mg, STEP 1, ≈ 14.9%). See the
[comparison](/vs/semaglutide-vs-tirzepatide).

**Q4. How long do tirzepatide treatment courses last in trials?**
SURMOUNT-1 ran 72 weeks [1]. SURMOUNT-4 demonstrated weight maintenance through
176 weeks of continued treatment, with regain after withdrawal [2]. Real-world
prescription typically continues indefinitely.

**Q5. What are the most common side effects of tirzepatide?**
Most commonly reported events from the Mounjaro and Zepbound labels are nausea
(12–33% dose-dependent), diarrhea (12–22%), vomiting (5–13%), and constipation
(6–11%) — most mild-to-moderate and transient during titration [9][10]. The labels
carry boxed warnings for thyroid C-cell tumor risk and additional warnings for
pancreatitis and gallbladder disease.

**Q6. Can tirzepatide be combined with semaglutide?**
**No.** Combining GLP-1 receptor agonists is not supported by any Phase 3 program
and is contraindicated by labeling [9][10]. The combination roughly doubles GI
adverse events without additive benefit.

**Q7. Does tirzepatide affect oral contraceptive efficacy?**
Yes — through delayed gastric emptying. The Zepbound label recommends switching to
a non-oral contraceptive or adding a barrier method for 4 weeks after initiation
and after each dose escalation [10].

**Q8. What is the SURMOUNT-OSA trial?**
SURMOUNT-OSA was a Phase 3 trial (n ≈ 469) of tirzepatide 10 / 15 mg weekly in
adults with obesity and moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea, showing
significant reduction in the apnea–hypopnea index over 52 weeks [7]. Zepbound's
label was expanded in 2024 to include this indication.

**Q9. Does tirzepatide help heart failure?**
The SUMMIT trial in obesity-related heart failure with preserved ejection fraction
(HFpEF) reported improvements in KCCQ-CSS quality-of-life scores and 6-minute
walk distance with tirzepatide 15 mg over 52 weeks [8]. This is a quality-of-life
outcome rather than a mortality outcome.

**Q10. Who should not take tirzepatide?**
Per the Mounjaro and Zepbound labels, patients with personal/family history of
medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2 are contraindicated; the labels also caution
in pregnancy planning, pancreatitis history, severe GI disease, and combination
with insulin/sulfonylureas without dose reduction [9][10].

**Q11. Is compounded tirzepatide the same as Mounjaro or Zepbound?**
No. Compounded tirzepatide is prepared by 503A/503B pharmacies and is not the
FDA-approved product. The agency's stance on compounded tirzepatide has shifted
with the official shortage status.

**Q12. How does tirzepatide compare to retatrutide?**
Retatrutide adds glucagon receptor agonism to the GLP-1 + GIP combination —
described as a triple agonist. Phase 2 retatrutide produced ≈ 24% weight loss at
48 weeks in adults with obesity [retatrutide page]. Phase 3 (TRIUMPH) is ongoing
and retatrutide is **not yet FDA-approved**. See the
[retatrutide page](/peptides/retatrutide) for details.
