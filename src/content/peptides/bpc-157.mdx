## What is BPC-157?

BPC-157 is a stable pentadecapeptide — a 15-amino-acid sequence (Gly-Glu-Pro-Pro-Pro-Gly-
Lys-Pro-Ala-Asp-Asp-Ala-Gly-Leu-Val) derived from a fragment of human gastric juice
protein. It was first characterized by the research group of Predrag Sikirić at the
University of Zagreb in the early 1990s, and the published preclinical literature on
its tissue-protective and pro-healing effects now spans more than thirty years [1][2].
Researchers also refer to BPC-157 by its development code PL-14736.

BPC-157 is **not approved by the FDA for any human therapeutic indication**. In
September 2023, the FDA's compounding-bulks evaluation effectively restricted its use
in compounded medications [10]. The compound continues to be sold by research-chemical
suppliers labeled "for research use only" and is not intended for human consumption.

## How BPC-157 Works (Mechanism of Action)

### Pathways implicated in animal studies

Across the preclinical literature, BPC-157 has been reported to act on several repair
pathways simultaneously rather than through a single receptor:

- **Angiogenesis.** Activation of the VEGFR2-Akt-eNOS pathway, increasing capillary
  formation in injury sites [6].
- **Collagen and fibroblast biology.** Enhanced fibroblast outgrowth and survival via
  FAK–paxillin signaling, reported in transected Achilles tendon models [3].
- **Nitric oxide system modulation.** Counteracts both NO over- and under-production,
  consistent with a cytoprotective role rather than a pure agonist or antagonist [7].
- **Growth factor cross-talk.** Modulation of growth-factor expression at injury
  sites is reported across tendon, ligament, muscle, gut, and nerve models [4].

A canonical receptor for BPC-157 has not been definitively identified in the
published literature.

## Clinical & Preclinical Evidence

### Human studies

**Human evidence on BPC-157 is limited.** A 2025 systematic review of musculoskeletal
healing identified 35 preclinical studies and only 1 clinical study published between
1993 and 2024 [4]. A 2026 orthopaedic review reached the same conclusion: despite
substantial preclinical signal, "a significant lack of human clinical evidence"
supports use in orthopaedic indications [9].

## Dosing in Research Settings

> **Important.** The numbers below describe doses used in published research and
> dose ranges discussed in the research community, not recommendations. Decisions
> about peptide use must be made with a licensed physician.

### Reported dosing ranges

The peptides.ts dataset records a commonly-cited research-community subcutaneous
range of **250–500 µg, once daily**, with cycle lengths of 4–8 weeks. The 2025
systematic review documents that animal studies have used substantially wider ranges
across intraperitoneal, intragastric, and topical routes [4].

the published literature have used both once-daily and split twice-daily schedules

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1. What is BPC-157?**
BPC-157 (Body Protection Compound-157) is a synthetic 15-amino-acid peptide derived
from a protective fragment of human gastric juice protein, first characterised by
the Sikirić research group in the early 1990s. It has been studied across more than
three decades of preclinical work for tendon, ligament, gut, and vascular repair
[1][2]. It is not approved by the FDA for human use [10].

**Q2. What is the half-life of BPC-157?**
The commonly-cited subcutaneous half-life of BPC-157 in animal models is
approximately 4 hours, which is consistent with both once-daily and split twice-
daily research dosing schedules. The half-life has not been formally established in
humans.

**Q3. Can BPC-157 be taken orally?**
The published literature reports that BPC-157 is orally active in rat models, with
beneficial effects on the gastrointestinal tract observed even when administered
intragastrically [2]. Whether oral bioavailability translates to systemic effects
in humans has not been established in published controlled trials.

**Q4. How long do BPC-157 cycles last in research?**
Animal studies have used both single-dose and multi-week protocols; community-
practice cycle lengths typically run 4–8 weeks. Cycle length in published animal
research varies by injury model and route of administration [4].

**Q5. Does BPC-157 affect tendon healing?**
In rat models of transected Achilles tendon, BPC-157 has been reported to
accelerate healing, enhance tendon fibroblast outgrowth, and promote survival via
FAK–paxillin signalling [3]. A 2025 systematic review reached the same conclusion
across 35 preclinical studies of tendon, ligament, muscle, and bone repair [4].
Equivalent controlled human data is not yet published.

**Q6. What are the reported side effects of BPC-157 in studies?**
Controlled human safety data is limited. Pharmacovigilance from research-community
practice has reported mild lightheadedness (~5%), nausea especially with oral
administration (~3–5%), vivid dreams (~5%), and mild mood elevation (~8%). The 2026
orthopaedic primer notes that human safety data is insufficient to characterise the
full adverse-event profile [9].

**Q8. BPC-157 vs TB-500 — what's the research difference?**
BPC-157 is reported to act through angiogenesis, collagen biology, and nitric-oxide
modulation [6][7]. TB-500 acts through cytoskeletal actin sequestration. Both have
been studied in tissue-repair models; the combination is the most popular research-
community healing stack but has not been studied head-to-head in controlled trials.
See the [BPC-157 vs TB-500 comparison](/vs/bpc-157-vs-tb-500).

**Q9. What's the difference between subcutaneous and intramuscular BPC-157
administration in studies?**
Animal studies have used intraperitoneal, intragastric, intramuscular, and
subcutaneous routes [4]. The relative efficacy of each route in humans has not been
established in published controlled trials. Research-community practice often uses
subcutaneous administration for convenience.

**Q10. Has any human clinical trial of BPC-157 been published?**
The 2025 systematic review identified 35 preclinical studies and only 1 clinical
study published between 1993 and 2024 [4]. The 2026 orthopaedic primer concluded
that "a significant lack of human clinical evidence" supports use in orthopaedic
indications [9].

**Q11. Why was BPC-157 restricted by the FDA in 2023?**
In September 2023 the FDA's compounding-bulks evaluation under Section 503A added
restrictions that removed BPC-157 from the list of substances available for
compounded medications [10]. The agency cited insufficient safety and effectiveness
data for compounded use.

**Q12. Is BPC-157 safe to combine with anticoagulants?**
No formal drug-interaction studies have been published. A 2017 rat study reported
that BPC-157 modulated haemostasis without directly affecting platelet function
[6]; the 2026 cytoprotection review reached a similar conclusion [7]. Clinical
relevance to human patients on anticoagulants is not established, and the
orthopaedic primer recommends avoiding the combination outside formal research [9].
