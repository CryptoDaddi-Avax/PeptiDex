## What is TB-500?

TB-500 is a synthetic peptide derived from the active region of **Thymosin Beta-4
(Tβ4)** — a 43-amino-acid actin-sequestering peptide that is among the most
abundant intracellular proteins in mammalian cells. The TB-500 molecule sold by
research-chemical suppliers is most commonly the N-acetylated 17-23 fragment of
Tβ4, identified and characterised in anti-doping detection literature [6].

> **Naming caution.** "TB-500" and "Thymosin Beta-4" are sometimes used
> interchangeably in research-community literature but are not chemically
> identical. TB-500 is a synthetic fragment; recombinant Tβ4 (RGN-352, used in
> the Phase I safety study) is the full-length 43-amino-acid peptide [3]. Findings
> from full-length Tβ4 studies are commonly extrapolated to TB-500 in research-
> community practice; the extrapolation is plausible but not formally validated.

TB-500 is **not approved by the FDA** for any therapeutic indication. It is on
WADA's prohibited list and has been the subject of multiple anti-doping detection
publications [6].

## How TB-500 Works (Mechanism of Action)

### Actin biology and downstream effects

Thymosin Beta-4 is a small, intracellular actin-sequestering peptide. Its
proposed mechanism involves regulating the polymerisation/depolymerisation of
G-actin and F-actin, supporting cell migration, wound contraction, and tissue
remodelling [1]. Downstream effects reported in animal models include:

- Promotion of re-epithelialisation, collagen deposition, angiogenesis, and
  keratinocyte migration in dermal wound models [1][4].
- Activation of endogenous cardiac progenitor cells, supporting myocardial and
  vascular regeneration after systemic administration in mice [2].
- Reduced cardiac fibrosis and scar formation post-myocardial infarction,
  inhibiting ROCK1 signalling and promoting neovascularisation [5].

## Dosing in Research Settings

### Reported dosing

The Phase I safety study used multiple IV dose levels of recombinant Tβ4 [3]
(specific µg/kg values vary by cohort). Research-community practice for
subcutaneous TB-500 typically discusses a loading phase of 5 mg twice weekly for
4 weeks followed by a maintenance phase of 2.5 mg twice weekly — these schedules
are **not** validated trial doses.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1. What is TB-500?**
TB-500 is a synthetic peptide derived from the active region of Thymosin Beta-4
(Tβ4), an actin-sequestering peptide studied for tissue repair and wound healing.
The TB-500 sold by research suppliers is typically the N-acetylated 17-23
fragment of Tβ4 [6]. It is not FDA-approved.

**Q2. TB-500 vs Thymosin Beta-4 — are they the same thing?**
No. TB-500 is a synthetic fragment of Tβ4; recombinant full-length Tβ4 (43 amino
acids) is what was used in the Phase I safety study [3]. Findings from
full-length Tβ4 are commonly extrapolated to TB-500 in research-community
practice but the extrapolation is not formally validated.

**Q3. What is the half-life of TB-500?**
Reported short — likely measured in hours rather than days. The on-site dataset
cites approximately 2 hours; this should be verified against a primary source
before being treated as load-bearing.

**Q4. Does TB-500 actually heal injuries?**
Animal models report accelerated dermal wound healing, cardiac progenitor cell
mobilisation, and reduced cardiac fibrosis after MI [1][2][5]. The Phase I human
study established safety [3] but did not measure efficacy. Equivalent controlled
human efficacy data is not yet published.

**Q5. TB-500 vs BPC-157 — what's the research difference?**
TB-500 acts through cytoskeletal actin sequestration; BPC-157 acts through a
multi-pathway profile including angiogenesis, nitric-oxide modulation, and
collagen biology. Both are studied in tissue-repair contexts; the combination is
the most widely-discussed research-community healing pairing. See the
[BPC-157 vs TB-500 comparison](/vs/bpc-157-vs-tb-500).

**Q6. Is TB-500 detectable in drug tests?**
Yes — multiple published assays detect TB-500 / Tβ4 fragments in plasma and
urine using LC-MS/MS [6]. TB-500 is included in WADA's prohibited list.

**Q7. What dose was used in the Phase I safety study?**
The Phase I study used multiple IV doses of recombinant Tβ4 in healthy
volunteers; the safety profile was acceptable across the doses tested with no
dose-limiting toxicities reported [3].

**Q8. How long do TB-500 cycles last in research?**
Research-community practice typically discusses a loading-then-maintenance
schedule (e.g., 4 weeks loading at higher dose, then maintenance at lower dose).
Published clinical work has been short and dose-finding rather than cycle-based.

**Q9. Can TB-500 be combined with BPC-157?**
The combination is the most popular research-community healing stack but no
controlled human trial has been published. Use the
[interaction checker](/tools/interactions).

**Q10. Does TB-500 help cardiac recovery?**
Animal models report reduced cardiac fibrosis and scar formation post-MI [5] and
mobilisation of endogenous cardiac progenitor cells [2]. Equivalent controlled
human efficacy data is not yet published.

**Q11. Is TB-500 safe in cancer patients?**
Because the actin-biology mechanism affects cell migration and angiogenesis,
TB-500 research is conservatively excluded in patients with active or recent
malignancy. No human cancer-safety data is available.

**Q12. What's the difference between TB-500 and the Tβ4 used in clinical trials?**
The clinical trials used recombinant full-length Tβ4 (43 amino acids), produced
under cGMP for clinical use [3]. TB-500 sold by research-chemical suppliers is
typically a shorter synthetic fragment, often N-acetylated 17-23 [6], without
the regulatory and manufacturing controls of clinical-grade material.
