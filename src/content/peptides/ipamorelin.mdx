## What is Ipamorelin?

Ipamorelin is a pentapeptide (five amino acids) developed in the late 1990s as a
**selective** growth-hormone secretagogue — meaning it releases GH from the
pituitary gland through the ghrelin receptor (GHS-R1a) without the off-target
hormonal effects (cortisol, prolactin, ACTH, FSH, LH, TSH elevations) that limit
older GHRPs such as GHRP-6 and GHRP-2 [1].

Ipamorelin is **not approved by the FDA** for any therapeutic indication and is
classified as a research compound. In September 2023, the FDA's compounding-bulks
evaluation under Section 503A added restrictions affecting compounded peptide
hormone preparations including ipamorelin.

## How Ipamorelin Works (Mechanism of Action)

### Receptor binding and pathway

Ipamorelin is an agonist at the growth-hormone secretagogue receptor (GHS-R1a, the
ghrelin receptor) [1]. Activation in pituitary somatotrophs triggers GH release
through the canonical ghrelin-pathway second-messenger cascade. The selectivity of
ipamorelin — relative to older GHRPs — was demonstrated in the original Raun et
al. swine study, in which dose-dependent GH release occurred without measurable
elevations of ACTH, cortisol, prolactin, FSH, LH, or TSH [1].

### Human studies

Human evidence is limited to PK/PD work and small dose-finding studies. There is
no Phase 3 program in any indication.

## Dosing in Research Settings

> The numbers below describe schedules used in published research and discussed in
> research-community practice — **not recommendations**.

### Reported dosing

The Gobburu et al. PK/PD study used IV doses up to approximately 80 µg/kg in
healthy adults [2]. Research-community practice commonly discusses subcutaneous
microgram doses (200–300 µg per administration), often in 2–3 daily injections,
based on the short half-life — but these schedules are **not** validated trial
doses.

## Safety, Side Effects & Contraindications

### Reported adverse events

The original characterisation work emphasised the **selectivity** of ipamorelin
relative to older GHRPs — no measurable elevations of cortisol, prolactin, ACTH,
FSH, LH, or TSH at GH-releasing doses in swine [1]. The human PK/PD study did not
report dose-limiting toxicities at the doses studied [2]. Specific human adverse-
event incidence numbers have not been published in a controlled-trial format.

### Populations who should avoid

Conservative exclusions for any GH-secretagogue research include pregnancy,
paediatrics, active malignancy, and patients with diabetic complications because
sustained GH elevation can worsen insulin resistance.

### Drug interactions

No formal drug-interaction studies in humans have been published.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1. What is ipamorelin?**
Ipamorelin is a pentapeptide selective growth-hormone secretagogue acting on the
ghrelin receptor (GHS-R1a) [1]. Its key research feature is releasing GH without
measurably elevating ACTH, cortisol, prolactin, FSH, LH, or TSH at GH-releasing
doses in characterisation studies [1].

**Q2. What is the half-life of ipamorelin?**
The terminal plasma half-life in healthy adult humans is approximately 2 hours,
with peak GH response within ~0.67 hours of administration [2].

**Q3. Ipamorelin vs CJC-1295 — what's the difference?**
Ipamorelin acts at the ghrelin receptor (GHS-R1a); CJC-1295 acts at the GHRH
receptor. They are mechanistically complementary, which is why the two are
commonly combined in research-community practice. No controlled human study has
assessed the combination. See [`CJC-1295 vs Ipamorelin`](/vs/cjc-1295-vs-ipamorelin).

**Q4. Does ipamorelin raise cortisol?**
In the original characterisation study in swine, ipamorelin produced potent GH
release without measurable cortisol elevation — a key differentiation from older
GHRPs [1]. This selectivity advantage is the principal reason ipamorelin is
preferred over GHRP-6 / GHRP-2 in research-community practice.

**Q5. What dose was used in the human PK/PD study?**
Gobburu et al. used IV doses up to approximately 80 µg/kg in healthy adults [2].
Research-community practice typically discusses subcutaneous microgram doses
multiple times daily; these are not validated trial doses.

**Q6. Is ipamorelin detectable in drug tests?**
Yes. Multiple published assays detect GHS-R1a agonists including ipamorelin in
urine and plasma; ipamorelin is included in WADA's S2 peptide-hormone category.
{/* TODO: Cite WADA list. */}

**Q7. Does ipamorelin help bone density?**
In adult female rat models, ipamorelin produced increased bone mineral content
and countered glucocorticoid-induced bone loss [3]. Equivalent controlled human
data has not been published.

**Q8. How long do ipamorelin cycles last in research?**
Published clinical work has been short (PK/PD timeframes). Research-community
cycle lengths vary widely and are not based on controlled trials.

**Q9. Does ipamorelin affect appetite?**
Mild appetite increase has been reported in community pharmacovigilance.
Quantitative incidence data from controlled trials is not available.

**Q10. Can ipamorelin be combined with semaglutide?**
The on-site dataset records this combination as cautioned because GLP-1 agonism
may blunt GH-secretagogue effects in some animal data. No published human trial
has assessed the combination.

**Q11. Does ipamorelin have analgesic effects?**
A 2020 rat study reported ipamorelin attenuated visceral and somatic nociception
in non-inflammatory pain models, mediated through peripherally-restricted
ghrelin-receptor activation [6]. Equivalent human data has not been published.

**Q12. How does ipamorelin fit alongside semaglutide for weight management?**
Ipamorelin is **not** a weight-loss agent. The on-site dataset cautions
combination with GLP-1 agonists. For weight management, the FDA-approved options
are semaglutide and tirzepatide; see those profile pages for primary data.
