## What is Semaglutide?

Semaglutide is a 31-amino-acid analog of human glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1),
modified to resist degradation by dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) and to bind albumin
in plasma — producing a half-life long enough to support once-weekly subcutaneous
dosing or once-daily oral dosing. It is sold under three FDA-approved brand names:

- **Ozempic** (subcutaneous, T2D) — approved by the FDA in 2017 [9].
- **Rybelsus** (oral, T2D) — the first oral GLP-1 receptor agonist [4].
- **Wegovy** (subcutaneous 2.4 mg, chronic weight management) — approved 2021 [8].

Unlike most peptides covered on PeptiDex, semaglutide is a **prescription medicine
with a complete FDA labeling, robust Phase 3 evidence, and long-term real-world
safety data**. It is not a research-only compound.

## How Semaglutide Works (Mechanism of Action)

### Receptor binding and pathway

Semaglutide is a selective GLP-1 receptor agonist. GLP-1 receptors are expressed on
pancreatic beta cells, the gastrointestinal tract, and several CNS regions including
the arcuate nucleus and area postrema [4]. Activation produces:

- Glucose-dependent insulin secretion from pancreatic beta cells.
- Suppression of glucagon secretion from alpha cells.
- Slowed gastric emptying.
- Reduced appetite, mediated through hypothalamic and brainstem GLP-1 receptor
  populations.

The label-documented effect on cardiovascular outcomes — independent of glucose
control — appears to be mediated by additional anti-inflammatory and vascular
effects of chronic GLP-1 receptor activation [2].

### Pharmacokinetics summary

| Property | Reported value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Half-life (subcutaneous) | ≈ 168 h (≈ 1 week) | [8][9] |
| Time to steady state | ~4–5 weeks | [8] |
| Bioavailability (subcutaneous) | ≈ 89% | [8] |
| Bioavailability (oral, with SNAC absorption enhancer) | < 1%, sufficient for therapeutic effect | [4] |
| Primary clearance | Proteolytic, with renal/biliary excretion of fragments | [8] |
| Plasma protein binding (albumin) | > 99% | [8] |

The very long half-life is the basis for once-weekly subcutaneous dosing.

## Clinical & Preclinical Evidence

### Human studies

Semaglutide has one of the largest Phase 3 evidence bases of any anti-obesity or
anti-diabetic medication.

| Study (year) | Population | n | Dose | Duration | Key outcome | Citation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| STEP 1 (2021) | Adults with obesity, no T2D | 1,961 | 2.4 mg SC weekly | 68 wk | −14.9% body weight vs −2.4% placebo | [1] |
| STEP program (pooled) | Adults with overweight/obesity | several thousand | 2.4 mg SC weekly | 68–104 wk | 14.9–17.4% mean weight loss; improved cardiometabolic markers | [3] |
| SELECT (2023) | Overweight/obese adults with prior CV disease, no T2D | 17,604 | 2.4 mg SC weekly | mean 39.8 mo | 20% relative reduction in MACE (HR 0.80) | [2] |
| SUSTAIN 3 (2018) | Adults with T2D | ~800 | 1.0 mg SC weekly vs exenatide ER | 56 wk | Greater HbA1c and weight reduction vs exenatide | [5] |
| PIONEER (2019) | Adults with T2D | several thousand across program | Oral 7 mg / 14 mg | 26–78 wk | Significant HbA1c and weight reduction; first oral GLP-1RA | [4] |
| SURPASS-2 (2021) | Adults with T2D | 1,879 | 1.0 mg SC weekly (semaglutide arm) | 40 wk | −1.86% HbA1c, −5.7 kg weight (vs tirzepatide superior) | [6] |

### Animal and preclinical studies

Preclinical work informed the molecular design of semaglutide (Lys26 fatty-acid
attachment for albumin binding, two amino-acid substitutions to resist DPP-4) but
the clinical program is now far more relevant for clinical decision-making than the
animal data.

## Dosing in Research Settings

> The wording below is calibrated to the FDA labels [8][9]. **A licensed clinician
> directs prescription-medicine dosing.** This page describes regimens used in the
> approved label and Phase 3 program — it is not a recommendation.

### Reported dosing — subcutaneous (Wegovy, chronic weight management) [8]

The FDA label uses a 16-week titration schedule starting at 0.25 mg subcutaneous
once weekly, escalating to a maintenance dose of 2.4 mg once weekly:

| Weeks 1–4 | Weeks 5–8 | Weeks 9–12 | Weeks 13–16 | Maintenance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 mg | 0.5 mg | 1.0 mg | 1.7 mg | 2.4 mg |

### Reported dosing — subcutaneous (Ozempic, T2D) [9]

| Weeks 1–4 | Weeks 5–8 | Maintenance | Maximum |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 mg | 0.5 mg | 0.5–1.0 mg | 2.0 mg |

### Reported dosing — oral (Rybelsus, T2D) [4]

3 mg once daily for 30 days, then 7 mg daily; may escalate to 14 mg daily after
≥ 30 days at 7 mg.

### Half-life and timing

The ≈ 168-hour subcutaneous half-life is the basis for once-weekly dosing on the
same day each week [8].

### Reconstitution

Branded semaglutide (Wegovy, Ozempic) is supplied as a pre-filled, ready-to-use
pen and **does not require reconstitution**.

> **Compounded semaglutide.** Pharmacy-compounded semaglutide became widely
> available during the FDA shortage period for branded products. As of late 2024
> the FDA declared the shortage resolved, and the agency has stated that
> compounded semaglutide should not be substituted for the FDA-approved product
> outside of the narrow circumstances permitted under sections 503A/503B.

> **Do not combine with other GLP-1 receptor agonists** (tirzepatide, dulaglutide,
> liraglutide, exenatide, retatrutide). Concurrent use is not supported by any
> Phase 3 program and the FDA label warns against it [8].

## Safety, Side Effects & Contraindications

### Reported adverse events

Pulled from the Wegovy label [8] and STEP program data [1][3]:

| Event | Frequency in STEP 1 (sema 2.4 mg vs placebo) | Severity | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nausea | 44.2% vs 16.1% | mild–moderate, transient | [1][8] |
| Diarrhea | 31.5% vs 15.9% | mild–moderate | [1][8] |
| Vomiting | 24.8% vs 6.6% | mild–moderate | [1][8] |
| Constipation | 23.4% vs 9.5% | mild | [1][8] |
| Acute pancreatitis | uncommon, label warning | serious | [8] |
| Gallbladder disease | increased vs placebo | moderate–serious | [1][8] |

### Populations who should avoid

The Wegovy and Ozempic labels carry a **boxed warning** for risk of thyroid C-cell
tumors based on rodent data; semaglutide is contraindicated in patients with a
personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or in patients with
Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2) [8][9]. Other contraindicated
or cautioned populations per label:

- Pregnancy (use only if benefit clearly outweighs risk; the label recommends
  discontinuation ≥ 2 months prior to planned conception due to long half-life) [8].
- History of pancreatitis [8].
- Severe renal impairment — limited data; use with caution [8].
- Severe hepatic impairment — limited data [8].

### Drug interactions

Slowed gastric emptying may affect absorption of concomitant oral medications,
particularly those with narrow therapeutic indices [8]. Concurrent insulin or
sulfonylurea use increases hypoglycemia risk; dose reduction of the secretagogue
is recommended [8][9].

## Legal & Regulatory Status

### FDA status

**Approved.** Ozempic (2017) and Rybelsus (2019) for adults with type 2 diabetes;
Wegovy (2021) for chronic weight management in adults with BMI ≥ 30, or BMI ≥ 27
with at least one weight-related comorbidity [8][9]. SELECT label expansion (2024)
added a cardiovascular risk reduction indication for adults with overweight or
obesity and established cardiovascular disease.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1. What is semaglutide?**
Semaglutide is a long-acting GLP-1 receptor agonist FDA-approved as Ozempic and
Rybelsus for type 2 diabetes and as Wegovy for chronic weight management [8][9].
Once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide 2.4 mg produced ≈ 14.9% mean weight loss in
the STEP 1 trial at 68 weeks [1] and reduced major adverse cardiovascular events
by 20% in the SELECT trial [2].

**Q2. What is the half-life of semaglutide?**
The subcutaneous half-life is approximately 168 hours (about one week), which is
the pharmacological basis for once-weekly dosing [8]. Steady state is reached in
roughly 4–5 weeks. The very long half-life means clearance after the last dose is
gradual; the FDA label recommends discontinuation at least two months before
planned conception [8].

**Q3. Can semaglutide be taken orally?**
Yes — Rybelsus is an FDA-approved oral semaglutide formulation that uses the
absorption enhancer SNAC (sodium N-(8-(2-hydroxybenzoyl)amino)caprylate) to deliver
sufficient peptide across the gastric mucosa [4]. It is dosed once daily and was
the first oral GLP-1 receptor agonist approved.

**Q4. How long do semaglutide treatment courses last in trials?**
The Phase 3 STEP program studied semaglutide for 68 weeks for chronic weight
management [1][3]. SELECT followed participants for an average of 39.8 months [2].
Real-world prescription typically continues indefinitely — STEP-extension and
withdrawal data show that weight regain occurs after discontinuation.

**Q5. What are the most common side effects of semaglutide?**
The most commonly reported events in STEP 1 at 2.4 mg were nausea (44.2%),
diarrhea (31.5%), vomiting (24.8%), and constipation (23.4%), most mild-to-moderate
and transient during titration [1][8]. The Wegovy label carries a boxed warning for
thyroid C-cell tumor risk based on rodent data and notes increased risk of
gallbladder disease and acute pancreatitis [8].

**Q6. Does semaglutide reduce cardiovascular events?**
In the SELECT trial — 17,604 adults with overweight or obesity, established
cardiovascular disease, and no diabetes — semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly reduced the
composite primary endpoint of cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial
infarction, or non-fatal stroke by 20% (HR 0.80) over a mean of 39.8 months [2].

**Q7. Who should not take semaglutide?**
Per the Wegovy and Ozempic labels, patients with a personal or family history of
medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2 are contraindicated; the label also lists
hypersensitivity, pregnancy planning, and history of pancreatitis as cautions
[8][9]. A licensed clinician should review individual contraindications.

**Q8. Semaglutide vs Tirzepatide — what's the difference?**
Both are FDA-approved long-acting incretin therapies, but tirzepatide adds GIP
agonism to GLP-1 agonism [6]. In SURPASS-2 (T2D), tirzepatide produced greater
HbA1c and weight reduction than semaglutide [6]. Phase 3 weight-loss outcomes are
larger for tirzepatide (≈ 22.5% in SURMOUNT-1) than semaglutide (≈ 14.9% in STEP
1) [1]. See the [comparison page](/vs/semaglutide-vs-tirzepatide).

**Q9. Is compounded semaglutide the same as Wegovy or Ozempic?**
No. Compounded semaglutide is prepared by 503A/503B pharmacies and is not the
FDA-approved product. The FDA's stance on compounded semaglutide has shifted as
the official shortage status has changed.

**Q10. Does semaglutide cause muscle loss?**
Weight loss from semaglutide includes both fat and lean tissue, similar to other
caloric-deficit weight-loss interventions. The Wegovy label and STEP body-
composition substudies report that fat mass loss exceeds lean mass loss. Resistance
training and adequate protein intake during treatment are commonly recommended.

**Q11. Can semaglutide be combined with tirzepatide or other GLP-1 agonists?**
**No.** Combining GLP-1 receptor agonists is not supported by any Phase 3 program,
is contraindicated by labeling [8], and roughly doubles the GI adverse-event risk
without additive benefit.

**Q12. How long does semaglutide stay in the body after the last dose?**
With a half-life of approximately 168 hours, semaglutide takes about 5 weeks to
clear to ~3% of peak concentration, and this is the basis for the FDA label
recommendation to discontinue at least 2 months before planned conception [8].
