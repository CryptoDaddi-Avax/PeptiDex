## What is MK-677?

MK-677 (also called **ibutamoren**, **ibutamoren mesylate**, **L-163,191**, and
the trade name **Nutrobal**) is a **non-peptide, orally bioavailable**
ghrelin-receptor (GHS-R1a) agonist. The molecule was developed by Merck in the
mid-1990s and tested in a series of clinical studies for GH/IGF-1 modulation,
body composition, sleep, and bone formation. Unlike the injectable peptide
GHRPs (ipamorelin, GHRP-2, GHRP-6), MK-677 is a small synthetic non-peptide
molecule absorbed orally with sustained ~24-hour exposure [1][2].

MK-677 is **not approved by the FDA** for any therapeutic indication. It is sold
by research-chemical suppliers labeled "for research use only."

---

## How MK-677 Works (Mechanism of Action)

### Receptor binding and pathway

MK-677 is a non-peptide agonist at the growth-hormone secretagogue receptor
(GHS-R1a, the ghrelin receptor). Activation drives pulsatile GH release from
pituitary somatotrophs through the canonical ghrelin-pathway second-messenger
cascade. The downstream effect is sustained elevation of IGF-1 from hepatic
secretion in response to GH receptor binding [1][2].

In the published RCT literature, MK-677 raised GH and IGF-1 to young-adult
levels in healthy elderly subjects without affecting cortisol, prolactin, ACTH,
insulin, or thyroid hormones at standard doses [1].

### Pharmacokinetics summary

| Property | Reported value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Half-life (oral) | ≈ 24 h | [1] |
| Time to peak plasma concentration | hours after oral dose | [1] |
| Bioavailability (oral) | High, sufficient for daily dosing | [1] |

The 24-hour half-life supports once-daily oral dosing — the principal
distinguishing feature of MK-677 vs the injectable GHRPs.

## Clinical & Preclinical Evidence

### Human studies

MK-677 has the **most substantial published human RCT base** of any peptide on
this site outside the FDA-approved GLP-1 class. Selected pivotal studies:

| Study (year) | Population | n | Dose | Duration | Key outcome | Citation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chapman et al. (1996) | Healthy elderly | small | 25 mg PO daily | 2 weeks | GH and IGF-1 restored to young-adult levels; no effect on cortisol, prolactin, insulin, thyroid | [1] |
| Nass et al. (2008) | Healthy elderly | 65 | 25 mg PO daily vs placebo | 2 years | +1.1 kg fat-free mass; GH/IGF-1 elevated; fasting glucose +0.3 mmol/L; BMD unchanged | [2] |
| Svensson et al. (1998) | Obese males | small | 25 mg PO daily | 8 weeks | Significant increase in GH, IGF-1, fat-free mass; transient appetite increase; mild insulin resistance | [3] |
| Murphy et al. (1998) | Healthy young volunteers on calorie restriction | small | MK-677 | weeks | Reversed diet-induced nitrogen wasting; potent anti-catabolic effect | [4] |
| Copinschi et al. (1997) | Healthy young & older subjects | small | MK-677 | weeks | Stage IV (deep) sleep duration +50%; REM sleep +20%; correlated with overnight GH | [5] |
| Murphy et al. (2001) | Postmenopausal osteoporotic women | larger | MK-677 ± alendronate | 12 months | Increased osteocalcin and bone-specific alkaline phosphatase | [6][7] |

### Animal and preclinical studies

The clinical RCT base is now substantially more relevant for understanding MK-677
than the animal characterisation studies. Older preclinical and pharmacology
work supported the molecule's selection for human development.

## Dosing in Research Settings

> The numbers below describe schedules used in published research — not
> recommendations.

### Reported dosing

The pivotal published trials used **25 mg orally once daily** [1][2][3][6].
Dose-ranging trials have included 10 mg and 50 mg arms; 25 mg has been the most
extensively studied maintenance dose.

### Half-life and timing

The ~24-hour half-life supports once-daily dosing. The published studies
typically dosed in the evening to take advantage of the natural overnight
GH-secretion peak [5].

### Reconstitution

MK-677 is taken **orally** and does not require reconstitution. It is most
commonly supplied as oral capsules or as a flavoured oral solution.

## Safety, Side Effects & Contraindications

### Reported adverse events

MK-677 has been studied in long-duration RCTs (up to 2 years) [2], so the
adverse-event profile is better characterised than for most research-only
peptides. Reported events include:

| Event | Reported in trials | Severity | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Increased appetite | common | mild | [3] |
| Mild fluid retention / oedema | reported | mild | [2] |
| Increased fasting glucose | small mean increase (≈ 0.3 mmol/L over 2 years) | mild–moderate | [2] |
| Decreased insulin sensitivity | reported | mild | [3] |
| Lethargy / muscle pain | reported in some studies | mild | TODO source |
| Mild congestive heart failure (one subgroup) | reported in elderly trial | serious | [2] |

The Nass et al. 2-year trial reported a small number of cases of congestive
heart failure in older participants — the authors flagged this as a caution that
deserves attention in any prolonged use [2].

### Populations who should avoid

Conservative exclusions based on the published literature:

- Diabetes or pre-diabetes (because of insulin sensitivity reduction) [3].
- Heart failure or significant cardiovascular disease [2].
- Pregnancy and paediatrics (no safety data).
- Active malignancy (because IGF-1 elevation has been hypothesised to support
  tumour proliferation, though this has not been demonstrated clinically).

### Drug interactions

No formal drug-interaction studies in humans have been published. Concurrent
use with insulin or sulfonylureas could be expected to require attention given
MK-677's modest effect on glucose handling.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1. What is MK-677?**
MK-677 (ibutamoren, L-163,191) is a non-peptide oral ghrelin-receptor agonist
that stimulates pulsatile GH release from the pituitary, producing sustained
elevation of GH and IGF-1 with once-daily dosing [1][2].

**Q2. What is the half-life of MK-677?**
Approximately 24 hours, supporting once-daily oral dosing [1]. This is the
principal distinguishing feature of MK-677 vs the injectable GHRPs.

**Q3. MK-677 vs ipamorelin — what's the difference?**
Both bind the ghrelin receptor (GHS-R1a). MK-677 is non-peptide and orally
bioavailable with a 24-hour half-life; ipamorelin is a peptide given by
subcutaneous injection with a 2-hour half-life. The strongest evidence base
for MK-677 includes a 2-year randomized trial (n=65) [2]; ipamorelin's evidence
is mostly characterisation and PK work. See
[`MK-677 vs Ipamorelin`](/vs/mk-677-vs-ipamorelin).

**Q4. Does MK-677 cause water retention?**
Mild fluid retention has been reported in published RCTs, particularly during
the first weeks of treatment [2][3]. It typically subsides over time.

**Q5. Does MK-677 affect blood sugar?**
Yes — modestly. The Nass et al. 2-year trial reported a mean fasting-glucose
increase of approximately 0.3 mmol/L over 2 years; the Svensson et al. obese-
male trial reported mild insulin resistance [2][3]. Diabetic and pre-diabetic
patients should treat this finding seriously when discussing MK-677 with a
clinician.

**Q6. Does MK-677 actually build muscle?**
The Nass et al. 2-year trial reported approximately +1.1 kg of fat-free mass
in healthy elderly adults at 25 mg/day [2]. Whether this translates to similar
gains in younger trained adults has not been studied in a controlled trial.

**Q7. Does MK-677 improve sleep?**
Yes — the Copinschi et al. trial reported a 50% increase in stage IV (deep)
sleep duration and a 20% increase in REM sleep with MK-677, correlated with
overnight GH secretion [5]. This is a well-validated effect.

**Q8. What was the dose used in the Nass et al. 2-year trial?**
25 mg orally once daily for 2 years in 65 healthy elderly adults [2]. This is
the most extensive long-duration safety dataset for the molecule.

**Q9. Does MK-677 cause heart failure?**
The Nass et al. trial reported a small number of cases of congestive heart
failure in older participants [2]. The authors flagged this as a caution
deserving attention in any prolonged use, particularly in elderly populations.
Patients with pre-existing cardiovascular disease should not use MK-677 outside
a closely supervised research setting.

**Q10. Is MK-677 detectable in drug tests?**
Yes. MK-677 is included in WADA's prohibited list under the GH-secretagogue
category, and detection methods exist.

**Q11. How long do MK-677 cycles last in research?**
Published RCTs have used durations from 2 weeks (Chapman) [1] to 2 years (Nass)
[2] to 12 months (postmenopausal bone study) [6]. Long-duration use has
established the safety profile but also identified the cautions noted above.

**Q12. Why is MK-677 popular despite not being approved?**
MK-677 is the only oral GH secretagogue with a substantial multi-year RCT
evidence base [2]. The combination of oral administration, once-daily dosing,
and well-characterised effects on lean mass and sleep makes it heavily
discussed in research-community practice. The not-approved status reflects
that Merck did not pursue final regulatory approval for any indication.
